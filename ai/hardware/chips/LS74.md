# 74LS74 — Dual D-Type Positive-Edge-Triggered Flip-Flop

## Overview
The 74LS74 contains two independent positive-edge-triggered D-type flip-flops, each with individual asynchronous preset (~PRE) and clear (~CLR) inputs and complementary Q and ~Q outputs. On each rising edge of CLK, the Q output takes the logic level present at the D input. The asynchronous inputs override the clock: pulling ~PRE LOW forces Q HIGH immediately; pulling ~CLR LOW forces Q LOW immediately. Both overrides take effect regardless of the clock state. The device is a fundamental building block for registers, counters, synchronisers, and edge-detection circuits in Z80-based designs.

## Pinout
| Pin | Name | Type | Description |
|-----|------|------|-------------|
| 1 | ~1CLR | I | Flip-flop 1 asynchronous clear, active LOW |
| 2 | 1D | I | Flip-flop 1 data input |
| 3 | 1CLK | I | Flip-flop 1 clock input (captures D on rising edge) |
| 4 | ~1PRE | I | Flip-flop 1 asynchronous preset, active LOW |
| 5 | 1Q | O | Flip-flop 1 non-inverting output |
| 6 | ~1Q | O | Flip-flop 1 inverting output |
| 7 | GND | P | Ground (0 V) |
| 8 | ~2Q | O | Flip-flop 2 inverting output |
| 9 | 2Q | O | Flip-flop 2 non-inverting output |
| 10 | ~2PRE | I | Flip-flop 2 asynchronous preset, active LOW |
| 11 | 2CLK | I | Flip-flop 2 clock input (captures D on rising edge) |
| 12 | 2D | I | Flip-flop 2 data input |
| 13 | ~2CLR | I | Flip-flop 2 asynchronous clear, active LOW |
| 14 | VCC | P | Supply voltage (+5 V) |

## Notes
- Both ~PRE and ~CLR are asynchronous and override the clock; asserting both LOW simultaneously causes both Q and ~Q to go HIGH (indeterminate state that should be avoided).
- Setup time for D before the clock edge is typically 20 ns; hold time is typically 5 ns.
- Typical propagation delay from CLK to Q is 25 ns.
- Common Z80 applications include single-bit output latches, interrupt flip-flops, synchronising asynchronous signals to the bus clock, and divide-by-two clock circuits.
- The 74S74 (Schottky) variant is pin-compatible but offers faster propagation (~8 ns) at higher power dissipation.
